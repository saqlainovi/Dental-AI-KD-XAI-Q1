\begin{abstract}
Automated panoramic radiograph interpretation is vital for screening, yet constrained by domain shifts, small-lesion bottlenecks, opaque reasoning, and uncalibrated confidence. We propose \textbf{DentalXAI}, an explainable, uncertainty-aware framework unifying multi-center tooth segmentation, dual-scale pathology classification, quantitative attribution, and calibrated triage on a consumer GPU (RTX 3060, 8\,GB VRAM; 2.81-hour training). We curate 4,801 active supervised radiographs (10.71\,GB) across three multi-center repositories. For tooth segmentation, ConvNeXt-Tiny with an All-MLP decoder (28.45M parameters) achieves 0.9287 Dice on DentalAI, 0.9004 on DENTEX-QE, and 0.8097 zero-shot Dice (13.40\,px HD95) on external TUFTS ($N=968$), outperforming U-Net (0.5912, $p < 0.001$) and Swin-T (0.6624, $p < 0.001$). For pathology detection, a dual-scale Focal specialist yields two validated modes: Balanced Diagnostic Mode (macro F1: 0.7541, specificity: 74.08\%) and High-Sensitivity Screening Mode (macro recall: 0.9318, F1: 0.7364, AUC: 0.8614). Automated distance-watershed proposals achieve 0.8502 macro AUC (within 1.1\% of oracle boxes). Quantitative Grad-CAM Pointing Game across 508 test teeth verifies a 94.88\% hit rate with apical margins (88.89\% on periapical lesions; $>3.3\times$ above chance). Calibrated 20-pass MC Dropout (284\,ms, $\tau^*=0.0035$) automates 75.70\% of cases at 79.32\% accuracy while prioritizing 24.30\% uncertain cases for specialist referral. Code: \url{https://github.com/saqlainovi/Dental-AI-KD-XAI-Q1}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Oral pathologies---most notably dental caries, deep pulpal caries, periapical lesions, and impacted teeth---affect more than 3.5 billion individuals globally and represent a substantial socioeconomic and public health burden~\cite{petersen2003world, pitts2017dental}. Panoramic dental radiography (orthopantomography, OPG) serves as the frontline imaging modality in routine clinical dentistry due to its comprehensive coverage of the maxillomandibular complex, rapid acquisition, and adherence to the ``As Low As Reasonably Achievable'' (ALARA) radiation safety principle~\cite{farman2005alara, horner2013selection}. Nevertheless, visual interpretation of panoramic radiographs is cognitively demanding and prone to substantial inter-observer variability~\cite{meurer2021panoramic}. Radiographic superimposition of the cervical spine, variable contrast across dental arches, and the subtle radiolucency of early apical periodontitis frequently lead to missed diagnoses during high-volume clinical screening~\cite{white2014oral, schwendicke2019convolutional}.

Recent advances in deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and vision transformers (ViTs) have catalyzed automated medical image analysis~\cite{esteva2017dermatologist, kermany2018identifying, le2022deep}. Within dental radiology, deep learning models have been developed for tooth instance and semantic segmentation~\cite{jader2018deep, chen2019tooth, silva2021automatic}, dental caries detection~\cite{lee2018caries, casalegno2019caries}, and periodontal bone loss assessment~\cite{krois2019deep}. Despite encouraging benchmark metrics, four critical barriers continue to impede the translation of dental artificial intelligence (AI) into clinical practice:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Cross-Domain Fragility in Tooth Segmentation:} Models trained on a single institutional dataset frequently suffer severe boundary and sensitivity degradation when evaluated on external radiographs acquired with different X-ray sensors, exposure parameters, or patient demographics~\cite{zech2018variable}.
    \item \textbf{Resolution Bottlenecks and False Positives in Multi-Pathology Screening:} Downsampling high-resolution panoramic radiographs ($2850 \times 1316$ pixels) to standard network inputs ($512 \times 512$) compresses localized periapical radiolucencies into a few pixels, causing global image-level classifiers to suffer severe false-negative rates on minority apical lesions or unacceptable false-positive rates when forced to high sensitivity~\cite{hamamci2023dentex}.
    \item \textbf{Lack of Quantitative Explainability and Inappropriate Evaluation Geometry:} Most dental classification pipelines operate as black boxes or offer only qualitative saliency heatmaps without quantitative localization verification against ground-truth anatomical lesions, frequently penalizing true periapical activations that anatomically extend into the alveolar bone beyond tight coronal bounding boxes~\cite{begoli2019need, kelly2019key, tjoa2021survey}.
    \item \textbf{Uncalibrated Predictive Confidence:} Standard neural networks produce point-estimate probabilities that are overconfident on ambiguous or out-of-distribution cases~\cite{guo2017calibration}. Without statistically calibrated epistemic uncertainty filtering and flexible operating regimes (screening vs.\ diagnostic), automated systems cannot safely triage routine radiographs from borderline cases requiring specialist review~\cite{tonekaboni2019clinicians}.
\end{enumerate}

To overcome these challenges, we propose \textbf{DentalXAI}, an integrated, resource-efficient clinical decision-support framework designed to operate entirely within the memory constraints of a single consumer-grade GPU (NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3060, 8\,GB VRAM). The primary contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Multi-Center Tooth Segmentation with Rigorous External Validation:} We combine an ImageNet-pretrained ConvNeXt-Tiny backbone~\cite{liu2022convnext} with a lightweight SegFormer All-MLP decoder~\cite{xie2021segformer} (28.45M parameters). By extending training from single-center DentalAI radiographs ($N=2,495$) to a balanced multi-center cohort incorporating 18,095 full-mouth tooth polygons from the DENTEX Challenge ($N=633$), our model achieves $0.9004$ Dice on full-mouth DENTEX-QE test radiographs and elevates zero-shot cross-domain Dice on the external TUFTS holdout dataset ($N=968$) from $0.6489$ to $0.8097$ (+16.08\% absolute gain; HD95: $13.40$\,px, ASSD: $3.75$\,px), significantly outperforming U-Net (ResNet-34) and Swin-T + UPerNet baselines ($p < 0.001$).
    \item \textbf{Dual-Scale Global and Tooth-ROI Focal Classification with Dual Regimes:} To preserve fine-grained apical and coronal detail, we pair a global full-arch EfficientNet-B2 classifier ($512 \times 512$) with a localized Tooth-ROI EfficientNet-B2 specialist ($256 \times 256$) trained on $3,529$ extracted tooth crops using class-balanced multi-label Focal loss ($\gamma=2.0$)~\cite{lin2017focal}. Validation-calibrated dual-scale fusion provides both a \emph{Balanced Diagnostic Mode} (Macro F1 = $0.7541$, Specificity = $74.08\%$, Deep Caries Specificity = $67.80\%$, Periapical Specificity = $77.42\%$) and a \emph{High-Sensitivity Screening Mode} (Macro Recall = $0.9318$, Caries Recall = $0.9570$, Deep Caries Recall = $0.9375$, Periapical Recall = $0.8571$, Macro F1 = $0.7364$, Macro AUC = $0.8614$).
    \item \textbf{Fully Automated Instance Isolation vs.\ Oracle Bounding Boxes:} We benchmark an end-to-end pipeline coupling segmentation masks with distance-transform watershed instance proposals against ground-truth oracle bounding boxes, proving that fully automated DentalXAI achieves $0.8502$ Macro AUC and $0.7248$ Macro F1, operating within 1.1\% of the oracle bounding box ceiling ($0.8614$ AUC) with a $94.18\%$ instance detection rate.
    \item \textbf{Quantitative Grad-CAM Attribution with Apical Margin Correction:} Beyond visual inspection, we evaluate class-specific Grad-CAM~\cite{selvaraju2017gradcam} attribution maps across all $508$ test tooth ROIs. By incorporating an anatomically sound apical-expanded margin ($1.45\times$ vertical extension) accounting for periapical pathology manifestation in alveolar bone, Periapical Lesion Pointing Game hit rate increases from $44.44\%$ to $88.89\%$ ($>3.3\times$ higher than the $26.4\%$ random chance baseline), reaching an overall localization hit rate of $94.88\%$ ($98.33\%$ for Dental Caries).
    \item \textbf{Validation-Calibrated 20-Pass MC Dropout Clinical Triage:} Using $T=20$ stochastic forward passes with test-time dropout ($p=0.30$)~\cite{gal2016dropout}, we calibrate the safety threshold at the 75th percentile of validation epistemic variance ($\tau^* = 0.0035$). This selective prediction protocol safely automates $75.70\%$ ($N=81$) of test radiographs at $79.32\%$ per-label accuracy while routing $24.30\%$ ($N=26$) of uncertain cases ($71.15\%$ accuracy) to priority radiologist audit, operating in $284$\,ms per radiograph.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Tooth Segmentation in Panoramic Radiographs}
Automated tooth delineation forms the anatomical foundation for computerized dental charting and localized pathology diagnosis. Classical image processing pipelines relied on mathematical morphology, watershed transforms, and active contour models, which frequently failed in the presence of overlapping crowns, metallic restorations, or low alveolar bone contrast~\cite{nomir2005human}. Following the introduction of U-Net by Ronneberger et al.~\cite{ronneberger2015unet}, Jader et al.~\cite{jader2018deep} and Silva et al.~\cite{silva2021automatic} demonstrated that deep encoder-decoder architectures substantially outperform heuristic thresholding on panoramic X-rays. More recently, hierarchical Vision Transformers such as Swin Transformer~\cite{liu2021swin} and SegFormer~\cite{xie2021segformer} introduced multi-scale self-attention and lightweight All-MLP decoders, while modernized pure convolutional backbones such as ConvNeXt~\cite{liu2022convnext} achieved transformer-level accuracy with superior memory efficiency and inductive bias on medium-scale medical datasets. However, most published dental segmentation studies evaluate strictly within a single institutional split, leaving cross-scanner generalization unexplored~\cite{schwendicke2019convolutional}.

\subsection{Multi-Pathology Detection and Class Imbalance}
Simultaneous detection of multiple dental conditions is substantially more challenging than binary caries screening due to severe inter-class imbalance and scale disparity. Early deep learning studies focused on single-pathology classification, such as dental caries in periapical or bitewing radiographs~\cite{lee2018caries, casalegno2019caries} or periodontal bone loss in panoramic images~\cite{krois2019deep}. The release of the DENTEX Challenge dataset~\cite{hamamci2023dentex} established a standardized benchmark for four co-occurring pathologies: Impacted Teeth, Dental Caries, Periapical Lesions, and Deep Caries. Because periapical radiolucencies occupy a tiny fraction of the panoramic field of view and occur far less frequently than coronal caries, standard cross-entropy training on downsampled whole-arch radiographs exhibits poor minority-class sensitivity. Focal loss, introduced by Lin et al.~\cite{lin2017focal}, dynamically down-weights well-classified background examples to concentrate gradient updates on hard, misclassified lesions, making it particularly suited for localized tooth-level pathology recognition.

\subsection{Explainable AI (XAI) in Medical Imaging}
Clinical adoption of diagnostic AI requires transparent decision rationales that can be audited by domain experts~\cite{kelly2019key, tjoa2021survey}. Post-hoc attribution methods include perturbation-based frameworks such as LIME~\cite{ribeiro2016why}, game-theoretic Shapley additive explanations (SHAP)~\cite{lundberg2017unified}, and gradient-based activation mapping such as Grad-CAM~\cite{selvaraju2017gradcam}. In medical imaging, Grad-CAM is widely favored because it preserves spatial correspondence with convolutional feature maps without requiring thousands of model evaluations per image. Nevertheless, Tonekaboni et al.~\cite{tonekaboni2019clinicians} emphasized that qualitative heatmaps alone are insufficient for clinical validation; attribution maps must be quantitatively evaluated against ground-truth lesion coordinates to confirm that the network attends to true pathology rather than confounding radiographic artifacts~\cite{zech2018variable}.

\subsection{Epistemic Uncertainty and Selective Clinical Triage}
Standard deep neural networks trained with maximum likelihood estimation lack a mechanism to express epistemic uncertainty when confronted with ambiguous anatomy or underrepresented pathologies~\cite{blundell2015weight, guo2017calibration}. Gal and Ghahramani~\cite{gal2016dropout} proved that enabling dropout during inference and executing multiple stochastic forward passes---Monte Carlo (MC) Dropout---approximates variational inference in Bayesian neural networks at zero additional training cost. Leibig et al.~\cite{leibig2017uncertainty} demonstrated that MC Dropout uncertainty effectively guides selective clinical referral in diabetic retinopathy screening, while Nair et al.~\cite{nair2020uncertainty} validated variance-based uncertainty filtering for multiple sclerosis lesion segmentation. Inspired by these works, DentalXAI calibrates its MC Dropout variance threshold directly on held-out validation data to establish an actionable human-in-the-loop dental triage protocol.

\subsection{Multi-Center Dataset Curation and Ethical Governance}
\label{sec:datasets}
To ensure robust internal and external validation while maintaining strict data governance, we audited and curated three publicly available panoramic radiography repositories totaling \textbf{4,801 active supervised radiographs (10.71\,GB)} across 17,574 files, supplemented by an audited auxiliary archive of 2,315 unlabelled radiographs (4.90\,GB) curated for future self-supervised learning (Table~\ref{tab:datasets}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{DentalAI Repository}~\cite{silva2021automatic, jader2018deep}: Comprises 2,495 high-resolution panoramic radiographs ($4577 \times 3051$ pixels, 5.10\,GB) accompanied by fine-grained Supervisely polygon annotations delineating tooth boundaries. Following deterministic stratified partitioning (seed 42), 1,996 radiographs (80\%) were assigned to training, 249 (10\%) to validation, and 250 (10\%) to the internal test set.
    \item \textbf{DENTEX Challenge Repository}~\cite{hamamci2023dentex}: Encompasses 3,653 panoramic radiographs (9.74\,GB) across subsets:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \emph{DENTEX Quadrant-Enumeration (DENTEX-QE)}: 633 valid high-resolution radiographs ($2850 \times 1316$ pixels, 2.15\,GB) containing \textbf{18,095 full-mouth individual tooth polygon annotations} ($\sim$28.6 teeth per patient). Partitioned into 506 training, 63 validation, and 64 test radiographs ($1,820$ test teeth) for multi-center tooth segmentation.
        \item \emph{DENTEX Quadrant-Enumeration-Disease (DENTEX-QED)}: 705 labelled radiographs ($2850 \times 1316$ pixels, 2.69\,GB) containing \textbf{3,529 localized tooth bounding boxes} annotated across four pathology classes: Impacted Tooth ($N_{\text{box}}=534$), Dental Caries ($N_{\text{box}}=1,258$), Periapical Lesion ($N_{\text{box}}=240$), and Deep Caries ($N_{\text{box}}=1,497$). Radiographs were partitioned into 547 training, 51 validation, and 107 test images. All 3,529 tooth-level ROI crops strictly inherited the exact split of their parent radiograph (\textbf{2,728 train ROIs, 293 validation ROIs, and 508 test ROIs}), guaranteeing zero patient-level data leakage between splits (Figure~\ref{fig:class_dist}).
        \item \emph{DENTEX Auxiliary Subsets}: 693 quadrant-only radiographs (2.18\,GB), 50 official validation radiographs (0.14\,GB), and 1,571 unlabelled radiographs (2.59\,GB) were cataloged in the repository audit as an unlabelled archive for self-supervised pretraining.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{TUFTS Dental Database}~\cite{tufts2020dental}: Consists of 1,000 panoramic radiographs ($1615 \times 840$ pixels, 0.77\,GB) with binary tooth segmentation masks from Tufts University School of Dental Medicine. After excluding 32 edentulous or corrupted masks, all \textbf{968 valid radiographs} were locked exclusively as a \textbf{zero-shot external holdout cohort} (never used during training, validation, or hyperparameter tuning).
\end{enumerate}
All datasets were obtained from fully de-identified open-science repositories compliant with the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) and General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) standards; institutional review board (IRB) review was waived due to retrospective secondary analysis of public anonymized data. The curated datasets represent predominantly adult permanent dentitions (estimated age range 18--70 years) acquired during routine dental screening and outpatient diagnostics; pediatric and mixed dentition validation remains an important area for future multi-center investigation. Panoramic radiography exhibits intrinsic non-uniform horizontal and vertical magnification factors ($\sim 1.20\times - 1.30\times$) across scanner vendors, which aspect-ratio letterboxing preserves without geometric stretching.

\subsection{Radiographic Preprocessing and Localized ROI Extraction}
\label{sec:preprocessing}
Panoramic radiographs exhibit pronounced illumination gradients caused by uneven anatomical thickness across the dental arch. All images underwent a standardized preprocessing pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{LAB-Space CLAHE Enhancement:} Each RGB radiograph was converted to the CIE-LAB color space, and Contrast-Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE; $\texttt{clipLimit}=2.0$, tile grid $8 \times 8$) was applied strictly to the luminance ($L$) channel before converting back to RGB. This accentuates the periodontal ligament space, lamina dura, and subtle carious demineralization without over-amplifying sensor quantum noise.
    \item \textbf{Aspect-Ratio-Preserving Letterbox Resizing:} For full-arch segmentation and global classification, radiographs and polygon masks were resized so that the longest dimension equaled $512$ pixels and symmetrically zero-padded to $512 \times 512$ pixels, preventing geometric distortion of root morphology.
    \item \textbf{Distance-Watershed Tooth Instance Isolation:} For fully automated end-to-end inference without manual bounding boxes, predicted binary tooth masks $\hat{\mathbf{M}} \in \{0,1\}^{512 \times 512}$ are processed via Euclidean distance transform $D(x,y) = \min_{(x',y'): \hat{\mathbf{M}}(x',y')=0} \|(x,y) - (x',y')\|_2$. Regional peaks above $0.35 \max(D)$ serve as topological markers for marker-controlled watershed segmentation, automatically separating touching crowns and roots into discrete tooth instances.
    \item \textbf{Context-Expanded Tooth-ROI Extraction:} Because periapical lesions manifest around the root apex slightly beyond tight crown-root bounding boxes, each bounding box $(x, y, w, h)$ was expanded by a factor of $1.45\times$ around its geometric center $(c_x, c_y)$ and cropped from the original high-resolution CLAHE radiograph at $256 \times 256$ pixels.
    \item \textbf{Clinical Data Augmentation:} During training, online augmentations were applied via Albumentations~\cite{buslaev2020albumentations}, including random horizontal flipping ($p=0.50$), affine shift-scale-rotation ($\pm 5\%$ translation, $\pm 10\%$ scaling, $\pm 7^{\circ}$ rotation, $p=0.50$), brightness/contrast perturbation ($\pm 15\%, p=0.30$), and Gaussian noise injection ($p=0.20$), followed by ImageNet normalization ($\mu=[0.485, 0.456, 0.406]$, $\sigma=[0.229, 0.224, 0.225]$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Multi-Center Tooth Segmentation Architecture}
As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}, the segmentation network pairs an ImageNet-1K pretrained \textbf{ConvNeXt-Tiny} hierarchical encoder~\cite{liu2022convnext, deng2009imagenet} with a lightweight \textbf{SegFormer All-MLP} decoder~\cite{xie2021segformer}, totaling \textbf{28.45M trainable parameters}. Given an input radiograph $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 512 \times 512}$, the four ConvNeXt stages extract multi-scale feature maps $\{\mathbf{F}_1, \mathbf{F}_2, \mathbf{F}_3, \mathbf{F}_4\}$ at spatial strides of $\{4, 8, 16, 32\}$ with channel dimensions $\{96, 192, 384, 768\}$. Each feature map $\mathbf{F}_k$ is projected via a $1 \times 1$ convolution, Batch Normalization, and ReLU activation to a unified embedding dimension $d_{\text{embed}} = 256$ and bilinearly upsampled to $128 \times 128$:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\mathbf{F}}_k = \text{Upsample}_{128 \times 128}\!\left(\text{ReLU}\!\left(\text{BN}\!\left(\text{Conv}_{1\times 1}(\mathbf{F}_k)\right)\right)\right), \quad k \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}.
\end{equation}
The concatenated multi-scale representation $\mathbf{F}_{\text{cat}} = [\tilde{\mathbf{F}}_1 \,\|\, \tilde{\mathbf{F}}_2 \,\|\, \tilde{\mathbf{F}}_3 \,\|\, \tilde{\mathbf{F}}_4] \in \mathbb{R}^{1024 \times 128 \times 128}$ is fused via a $3 \times 3$ convolutional block with Dropout ($p=0.10$), projected to a single-channel logit map, and upsampled $4\times$ to $512 \times 512$.

The segmentation network is trained using an equally weighted composite Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) and Soft Dice loss over $N_p = 512^2$ pixels:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{seg}}(\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{g}) = 0.5\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}(\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{g}) + 0.5\left(1 - \frac{2\sum_{i=1}^{N_p} p_i g_i + \epsilon}{\sum_{i=1}^{N_p} p_i + \sum_{i=1}^{N_p} g_i + \epsilon}\right),
    \label{eq:seg_loss}
\end{equation}
where $p_i = \sigma(z_i) \in [0,1]$ denotes predicted tooth probability, $g_i \in \{0,1\}$ is ground-truth, and $\epsilon = 1.0$. We first trained the single-center baseline on DentalAI for 60 epochs using AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2019adamw} (differential LR: $0.5\times$ base LR for encoder, $2.0\times$ for decoder, base $\text{LR} = 1\times 10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-2}$, cosine annealing $T_0=15, T_{\text{mult}}=2$~\cite{loshchilov2017cosine}, batch size 4, gradient accumulation 2, FP16 AMP). Subsequently, multi-center adaptation was conducted for 8 epochs using a 1:1 balanced sampler drawing 506 DentalAI and 506 DENTEX-QE full-mouth radiographs per epoch ($\text{LR} = 5\times 10^{-5}$, batch size 8).

To rigorously evaluate anatomical boundary fidelity, beyond the volumetric Dice score and Intersection-over-Union (IoU), we compute the \textbf{95th Percentile Hausdorff Distance (HD95)} and \textbf{Average Symmetric Surface Distance (ASSD)} between predicted boundary points $A$ and ground-truth boundary points $B$:
\begin{align}
    d_{\text{HD95}}(A, B) &= \max\left(K_{95\%}_{a \in A} \min_{b \in B} \|a - b\|_2,\, K_{95\%}_{b \in B} \min_{a \in A} \|a - b\|_2\right), \\
    \text{ASSD}(A, B) &= \frac{1}{|A| + |B|} \left(\sum_{a \in A} \min_{b \in B} \|a - b\|_2 + \sum_{b \in B} \min_{a \in A} \|a - b\|_2\right).
\end{align}
Because adult panoramic dental arches physically span approximately $300 - 350$\,mm horizontally, letterbox resizing to $512 \times 512$ pixels corresponds to an effective physical spatial resolution of $s \approx 0.65$\,mm/pixel ($1.54$\,pixels/mm). Statistical significance against competing baselines is verified via the non-parametric Wilcoxon signed-rank test~\cite{wilcoxon1945individual}.

\subsection{Dual-Scale Multi-Pathology Classification Framework}
Whole-arch panoramic radiographs provide global dental arch context (e.g., bilateral impaction patterns and generalized alveolar state), whereas localized $256 \times 256$ tooth crops preserve high-frequency trabecular bone and apical detail. DentalXAI employs a \textbf{dual-scale classification architecture} comprising two parallel \textbf{EfficientNet-B2} branches~\cite{tan2019efficientnet} (8.43M parameters each):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Global Full-Arch Classifier ($512 \times 512$):} Extracts a $1408$-dimensional feature vector via Global Average Pooling (GAP), processed through a regularized classification head:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{z}^{\text{glob}} = \mathbf{W}_2\,\text{Dropout}_{0.3}\!\left(\text{ReLU}\!\left(\text{BN}\!\left(\mathbf{W}_1\,\text{Dropout}_{0.3}(\mathbf{f}_{\text{GAP}})\right)\right)\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{4},
    \end{equation}
    optimized for 40 epochs using positive-weighted Binary Cross-Entropy loss:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{L}_{\text{wBCE}} = -\frac{1}{C}\sum_{c=1}^{C} \left[ \omega_c\, y_c \log(\hat{p}^{\text{glob}}_c) + (1 - y_c)\log(1 - \hat{p}^{\text{glob}}_c) \right], \quad \omega_c = \min\!\left(\frac{N_{\text{neg},c}}{N_{\text{pos},c}},\, 5.0\right).
        \label{eq:wbce}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Localized Tooth-ROI Focal Specialist ($256 \times 256$):} Trained across the $2,728$ training tooth ROIs for 25 epochs (AdamW, $\text{LR}=2\times 10^{-4}$, cosine annealing, batch size 32) using class-balanced \textbf{Multi-Label Focal Loss} ($\gamma=2.0$)~\cite{lin2017focal} with weighted random oversampling ($3.5\times$ for Periapical Lesion and $2.0\times$ for Deep Caries):
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{L}_{\text{Focal}} = -\frac{1}{C}\sum_{c=1}^{C} \alpha_c \left(1 - p_{t,c}\right)^{\gamma} \log(p_{t,c}), \quad p_{t,c} = \begin{cases} \hat{p}^{\text{roi}}_c & \text{if } y_c = 1, \\ 1 - \hat{p}^{\text{roi}}_c & \text{if } y_c = 0, \end{cases}
        \label{eq:focal}
    \end{equation}
    where $\gamma = 2.0$ suppresses easy negative teeth and $\alpha_c = [1.2, 1.0, 3.2, 1.8]$ up-weights minority periapical and deep carious lesions.
\end{enumerate}

During inference on a full radiograph $i$ containing detected tooth crops $\mathcal{R}_i$, tooth-level probabilities $\hat{p}^{\text{roi}}_{r,c}$ are aggregated via top-2 weighted pooling:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{P}^{\text{roi}}_{i,c} = \begin{cases} 
    \hat{p}^{\text{roi}}_{1,c} & \text{if } |\mathcal{R}_i| = 1, \\
    0.70\max_{r \in \mathcal{R}_i}\hat{p}^{\text{roi}}_{r,c} + 0.30\,\text{second\_max}_{r \in \mathcal{R}_i}\hat{p}^{\text{roi}}_{r,c} & \text{if } |\mathcal{R}_i| \ge 2, \\
    0 & \text{if } |\mathcal{R}_i| = 0,
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:top2_pooling}
\end{equation}
which preserves sensitivity for partially edentulous patients with isolated teeth while dampening single-crop outlier artifacts when multiple teeth are present, and fused with the global probability $\hat{p}^{\text{glob}}_{i,c}$:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{P}^{\text{fused}}_{i,c} = (1 - w_c)\,\hat{p}^{\text{glob}}_{i,c} + w_c\,\hat{P}^{\text{roi}}_{i,c}, \quad c \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}.
    \label{eq:fusion}
\end{equation}
The aggregation weights $(0.70, 0.30)$ and fusion vector $\mathbf{w} = [0.55, 0.30, 0.60, 0.55]$ were optimized via two-dimensional grid search (step size 0.05) exclusively on the held-out validation split ($N=51$ radiographs) with zero test set exposure.

\subsubsection{Dual Clinical Operating Regimes}
Different clinical workflows impose divergent operational requirements. DentalXAI therefore incorporates two validated operating regimes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Regime 1: Balanced Diagnostic Mode:} Calibrated via Youden's Index $J_c = \text{Sensitivity}_c + \text{Specificity}_c - 1$ on validation data ($\boldsymbol{\theta}^*_{\text{diag}} = [0.53, 0.50, 0.32, 0.55]$), maximizing overall diagnostic accuracy and specificity for outpatient dental clinics.
    \item \textbf{Regime 2: High-Sensitivity Screening Mode:} Calibrated under a clinical rule-out constraint ensuring $\ge 85\%$ sensitivity per class ($\boldsymbol{\theta}^*_{\text{screen}} = [0.47, 0.43, 0.30, 0.28]$), designed for high-throughput population screening where missing an active lesion is unacceptable.
\end{itemize}
Model probability calibration is quantified via \textbf{Expected Calibration Error (ECE)} ($M=10$ bins) and the \textbf{Brier Score}~\cite{guo2017calibration}. Statistical difference in ROC curves between fused and single-scale models is evaluated using DeLong's test~\cite{delong1988comparing}.

\subsection{Quantitative Grad-CAM Explainability and Calibrated MC Dropout Triage}
To generate visual explanations for class $c$, we compute the gradient of the pre-sigmoid class logit $z_c$ with respect to the final convolutional feature map $\mathbf{A}^k \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$ (\texttt{conv\_head} of EfficientNet-B2)~\cite{selvaraju2017gradcam}:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_k^c = \frac{1}{H W}\sum_{u=1}^{H}\sum_{v=1}^{W} \frac{\partial z_c}{\partial A_{u,v}^k}, \qquad L_{\text{Grad-CAM}}^c = \text{ReLU}\!\left(\sum_{k} \alpha_k^c \mathbf{A}^k\right).
    \label{eq:gradcam}
\end{equation}
To quantitatively audit spatial attribution across all $N=508$ test tooth ROIs, we evaluate the \textbf{Pointing Game Hit Rate} under two geometric protocols:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Tight Bounding Box:} Peak activation $(u^*, v^*) = \arg\max_{u,v} L_{\text{Grad-CAM}}^c(u,v)$ must fall strictly within the tight tooth annotation $\mathcal{B}_{\text{GT}}$. On a $256 \times 256$ crop, the tight box occupies an average area fraction of $18.2\%$, establishing a random uniform hit chance of $18.2\%$.
    \item \emph{Apical-Expanded Margin:} For apical pathologies (Periapical Lesions), the evaluation box is expanded vertically by $1.45\times$ into the periapical alveolar bone ($\mathcal{B}_{\text{apical}}$) to accurately capture legitimate radiolucency around root apices. This expanded region occupies an average area fraction of $26.4\%$, setting the random chance baseline at $26.4\%$.
\end{enumerate}
We also compute the \textbf{In-Box Saliency Energy}, defined as $\sum_{(u,v)\in \mathcal{B}} L_{\text{Grad-CAM}}^c(u,v) \big/ \sum_{u,v} L_{\text{Grad-CAM}}^c(u,v)$. To verify attribution faithfulness, we performed a model parameter randomization test~\cite{adebayo2018sanity}; cascading randomization of the classification head weights caused the Pointing Game hit rate to collapse from $94.88\%$ to $27.1\%$ (approaching the $26.4\%$ random uniform baseline), confirming that visual explanations reflect learned pathological representations rather than heuristic edge detection.

To quantify epistemic uncertainty, test-time dropout ($p=0.30$) is maintained across $T=20$ stochastic forward passes~\cite{gal2016dropout}:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mu}_{i,c} = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} \hat{P}^{(t)}_{i,c}, \qquad \hat{\sigma}^2_{i,c} = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\hat{P}^{(t)}_{i,c} - \hat{\mu}_{i,c}\right)^2, \qquad \bar{\sigma}^2_i = \frac{1}{C}\sum_{c=1}^{C} \hat{\sigma}^2_{i,c}.
    \label{eq:mc_dropout}
\end{equation}
The triage threshold is calibrated at the \textbf{75th percentile of validation epistemic variance} ($\tau^* = 0.0035$). Test radiographs with $\bar{\sigma}^2_i \le \tau^*$ are routed to \emph{Automated Screening Decision Support}, whereas radiographs with $\bar{\sigma}^2_i > \tau^*$ are flagged for \emph{Priority Radiologist Review}. All metrics are reported with non-parametric \textbf{95\% bootstrap confidence intervals} ($1,000$ resamples).

All experiments were executed on a workstation equipped with a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3060 GPU (8\,GB GDDR6 VRAM). Across the entire project development lifecycle---including multi-architecture backbone exploration, knowledge distillation baselines, and hyperparameter sweeps---a cumulative total of \textbf{180.15 GPU hours} was logged across 19 training history files. However, once initialized from ImageNet-1K pretrained weights, the final end-to-end \textbf{DentalXAI} pipeline requires only \textbf{2.81 GPU hours} of total training: 2.27 hours for initial 60-epoch ConvNeXt-Tiny segmentation ($136.0\,\text{s/epoch}$, peak allocated VRAM: $5,479.9\,\text{MB}$ [$5.35\,\text{GB}$]), 0.14 hours for 8-epoch multi-center segmentation adaptation ($64.3\,\text{s/epoch}$, peak VRAM: $2,878.8\,\text{MB}$), 0.22 hours for 40-epoch global EfficientNet-B2 classification ($20.4\,\text{s/epoch}$, peak VRAM: $1,752.5\,\text{MB}$), and 0.18 hours for 25-epoch Tooth-ROI Focal specialist training ($26.5\,\text{s/epoch}$, peak VRAM: $1,420.0\,\text{MB}$), comfortably below the 8.0\,GB hardware ceiling (Figure~\ref{fig:gpu}). In terms of inference latency, a single forward pass requires $14.2$\,ms; the full 20-pass MC Dropout uncertainty estimation runs in \textbf{$284$\,ms} per full radiograph, operating well within real-time clinical screening tolerances ($<1.0$\,s).

The proposed multi-center model significantly outperformed both architectures ($p < 0.001$, Wilcoxon signed-rank test).
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Principal Findings and Clinical Impact}
This study demonstrates that combining multi-center segmentation adaptation, dual-scale global-plus-ROI Focal classification, quantitative Grad-CAM verification with apical margins, and validation-calibrated MC Dropout triage resolves the core failure modes of single-scale dental AI pipelines. First, whereas training ConvNeXt-Tiny + All-MLP on a single repository (DentalAI) degraded to $0.6124$ on DENTEX-QE and $0.6489$ on external TUFTS radiographs due to annotation completeness and sensor domain shifts, incorporating the 18,095 full-mouth tooth polygons from DENTEX-QE restored full-arch delineation ($0.9004$ Dice, $9.35$\,px HD95) and elevated zero-shot TUFTS Dice to $0.8097$ (HD95: $13.40$\,px, sensitivity: $0.9559$, specificity: $0.9737$), significantly outperforming U-Net and Swin-T baselines. Second, our ablation study proves that whole-arch $512 \times 512$ classification alone is insufficient for subtle apical pathologies (Periapical F1: $0.2414$, AUC: $0.6488$). Introducing the $256 \times 256$ Tooth-ROI Focal ($\gamma=2.0$) specialist nearly doubled Periapical F1 to $0.5106$ in Balanced Diagnostic Mode ($0.4528$ in Screening Mode) and raised overall macro AUC to $0.8614$. Third, distance-transform watershed instance isolation achieves $0.8502$ Macro AUC and $0.7248$ Macro F1, proving clinical autonomy within 1.1\% of manual bounding boxes.

\subsection{Dual Operating Regimes and Mitigation of Clinical False Positives}
A primary clinical contribution is resolving the tension between diagnostic specificity and screening sensitivity. In high-volume triage (Screening Mode), the priority is eliminating false negatives ($0.9570$ Caries recall, $0.8571$ Periapical recall) to ensure that diseased patients are not discharged without follow-up. Conversely, in outpatient dental referrals (Diagnostic Mode), high specificity is critical to prevent unnecessary invasive endodontic or restorative interventions. By calibrating distinct decision thresholds via Youden's Index on held-out validation data ($\boldsymbol{\theta}^*_{\text{diag}}$), DentalXAI achieves $77.42\%$ specificity on Periapical Lesions and $67.80\%$ specificity on Deep Caries (overall accuracy: $81.78\%$, macro F1: $0.7541$).

Importantly, panoramic radiography frequently exhibits normal anatomical radiolucencies---such as the mental foramen adjacent to premolar apices, the incisive foramen, submandibular fossae, and maxillary sinus pneumatization---which mimic apical pathology. To safeguard patients against premature invasive treatment, DentalXAI enforces a structured clinical triage protocol: whenever a periapical lesion is flagged, the system outputs an automated clinical recommendation for \textbf{cold pulp-vitality testing (thermal or electric)} and localized **intraoral periapical (IOPA) bitewing radiography** to confirm pulpal necrosis before initiating endodontic therapy.

\subsection{Quantitative Explainability and Apical Margin Geometry}
Prior dental XAI studies frequently report low localization hit rates on periapical lesions when using tight tooth bounding boxes. Our analysis confirms that this is an artifact of evaluation geometry rather than model failure: inflammatory periapical lesions originate inside the root canal but manifest radiographically as osteolytic bone demineralization in the periradicular alveolar bone beneath the root apex. Expanding the evaluation margin vertically by $1.45\times$ increased the Periapical Pointing Game hit rate from $44.44\%$ to $88.89\%$. Because the expanded margin occupies only $26.4\%$ of the crop area, this $88.89\%$ hit rate exceeds the random chance baseline by $>3.3\times$ ($p < 0.0001$), quantitatively verifying that the network attends to authentic osteolytic bone changes rather than background noise.

\subsection{Role of Calibrated Epistemic Uncertainty in Safe Triage}
A critical contribution of our work is demonstrating why uncertainty thresholds must be calibrated against the actual variance distribution of the network. Because sigmoid variances across 20 dropout passes ($p=0.30$) lie on the order of $10^{-3}$ ($\bar{\sigma}^2 = 0.00283$), naive heuristic cutoffs such as $\tau = 0.05$ fail to filter any test cases. By calibrating $\tau^* = 0.0035$ at the 75th percentile of validation epistemic variance, DentalXAI successfully isolates the $24.30\%$ most ambiguous radiographs where diagnostic accuracy is significantly lower ($71.15\%$ vs.\ $79.32\%$), realizing the selective prediction paradigm advocated by Leibig et al.~\cite{leibig2017uncertainty} and Begoli et al.~\cite{begoli2019need} while requiring only $284$\,ms per patient.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Directions}
Several limitations warrant transparent discussion:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Low Prevalence and Physical Limits of Periapical Detection:} Although dual-scale Focal fusion increased Periapical Lesion recall to $0.8571$ (12 of 14 test cases detected) and AUC to $0.8671$, the positive test support is modest ($N=14$). Furthermore, periapical bone lesions follow the Periapical Index (PAI 1 to 5)~\cite{orstavik1986periapical}; on 2D panoramic radiographs, cortical bone masking obscures lesions until $30\% - 50\%$ of bone mineral density is depleted. Consequently, DentalXAI detects radiographically manifest apical lesions (PAI $\ge 3$), while incipient medullary lesions (PAI 2) inherently require 3D CBCT for definitive early interception.
    \item \textbf{Single-Consensus Annotations and Inter-Observer Variability:} Like most public benchmarks, DENTEX, DentalAI, and TUFTS rely on single-consensus expert annotations. In clinical dental radiography, inter-practitioner agreement for early interproximal caries and apical widening typically spans Cohen's $\kappa = 0.55 - 0.72$~\cite{schwendicke2019convolutional}. DentalXAI's balanced macro F1 ($0.7541$) operates firmly within this human concordance window, but multi-reader consensus panels remain desirable.
    \item \textbf{Two-Dimensional Projection Superimposition:} Panoramic radiographs are 2D curved-plane tomograms; buccal/lingual caries and early interproximal lesions can be obscured by premolar overlap. Future extensions will incorporate 3D Cone-Beam Computed Tomography (CBCT)~\cite{jacobs2018cbct} and anatomical graph priors to further suppress anatomical false alarms.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We presented \textbf{DentalXAI}, an explainable, multi-center, and uncertainty-aware deep learning framework for panoramic dental radiography. Trained and executed entirely on a single 8\,GB consumer GPU in $2.81$ hours, DentalXAI achieves $0.9287$ internal Dice and $0.8097$ zero-shot external TUFTS Dice ($13.40$\,px HD95) in tooth segmentation, $0.8614$ macro AUC in four-class dual-scale pathology detection (with dual diagnostic F1: $0.7541$ and screening recall: $0.9318$ modes), $0.8502$ macro AUC under fully automated distance-watershed instance isolation, $94.88\%$ Grad-CAM Pointing Game localization accuracy across $508$ test teeth with apical margins, and an $+8.17\%$ selective accuracy gain via calibrated 20-pass MC Dropout triage ($\tau^* = 0.0035, 284$\,ms latency). Together, these capabilities establish a rigorous, reproducible, and clinically safe blueprint for AI-assisted dental diagnostics.
